# Critical Accounting Estimates

The preparation of our consolidated financial statements requires
management to make estimates and assumptions that affect reported amounts.
We consider the following to be our most critical accounting estimates,
based on the degree of judgment involved and the potential impact on our
financial statements.

## Revenue recognition for bundled arrangements

Contracts that combine a Hardware unit with a Software & Services
subscription require us to allocate the total contract price among
performance obligations based on estimated standalone selling prices. Where
a component is not sold separately, we estimate its standalone selling
price using expected cost plus a margin, informed by pricing of similar
components in comparable arrangements. Changes in these estimates could
materially shift revenue between periods.

## Warranty reserves

We accrue for estimated warranty obligations at the time of shipment, based
on historical failure rates and estimated repair costs by product
generation. Because our Gen4 platform shipped in meaningful volume for the
first time in fiscal 2023, our warranty reserve for that platform relies
more heavily on engineering estimates than on our own historical claims
experience, which increases estimation uncertainty relative to our more
mature Gen3 platform.

## Stock-based compensation

We estimate the fair value of stock options using the Black-Scholes model,
which requires subjective assumptions including expected volatility and
expected term. We estimate volatility based on the historical volatility of
a peer group of publicly traded companies, since our own trading history is
limited.

## Goodwill impairment

We test goodwill for impairment annually and whenever events indicate that
the carrying value of a reporting unit may exceed its fair value. Estimating
fair value requires assumptions about future cash flows and an appropriate
discount rate, both of which involve significant management judgment.
